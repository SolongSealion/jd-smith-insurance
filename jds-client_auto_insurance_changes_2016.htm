Ontario auto insurance is changing

A few years ago the provincial government passed the Fighting Fraud and Reducing Automobile Insurance Rates Act to fight fraud and abuse in the system. The measures were put in place not only to combat fraud, but to give consumers greater choice.

In a nutshell, the regulations brought lower accident benefit limits in standard insurance policies, new options to increase coverage, new procedures for payment, and a mandatory winter tire discount (up to 5%) offered by insurance companies across Ontario.

The new changes in July 2026 build on these earlier ones. Some benefits that were standard are now optional. See what is changing in 2026, and talk to your broker about the options available to you.

Why does any of this matter?

Car insurance can feel like just another monthly expense, money you could spend elsewhere (on that vacation you need, for example). Perhaps it's only at renewal that you think about the bigger picture: insurance as a safety net that protects you against risk, and as a key part of your own and your family's welfare. If you, a friend or a loved one is in an accident, your outlook changes to one of appreciation. What would have happened if a policy hadn't been in place?

These changes are a chance to step back and reflect on what you really need:

  • Does your employer provide a group insurance plan? Look into what it covers. Most benefit packages are limited to $500 a year per practitioner, which can run out quickly, even after a minor accident.
  • Do children or elderly parents depend on you? Who would care for them if you were injured?
  • Who would run the household? If you had to hire someone to take on your household responsibilities, how would you pay for it?

Standard policies are just that: standard. They don't include benefits like dependant care, which pays the extra cost of caring for your dependants. While auto insurance is becoming more affordable, gaps may exist depending on your lifestyle. The good news is that you can fill those gaps by increasing coverage or adding options for specific areas of your life. Buying the right coverage is an investment in your family, your children and your parents. Ultimately, it's an investment in your lifestyle.

Customize your auto insurance

With the changes to auto insurance in Ontario, it's the right time to look again at the policy you may not have thought about in years. Your insurance company will send you an update in the mail, and we're here to follow up with a conversation about your needs.

Consider more than what's offered in a standard policy. There are good options depending on your lifestyle. Think about updating your policy if:

You're a caregiver
You're the primary caregiver of young children, elderly parents or disabled family members.
You earn over $30,000
You earn more than $30,000 a year.
You're a snowbird
You spend part of the year away from Ontario.
You run the household
You're responsible for housekeeping or home maintenance.
You have a pre-existing injury
It needs regular treatment, like physiotherapy or massage.
Auto is your only coverage
You don't have a group plan through an employer or spouse.

How far do standard benefits go?

Catastrophic injury
$1,000,000
For medical, rehabilitation and attendant care, e.g. loss of a limb, paraplegia or quadriplegia.
Less severe injury
$65,000
For injuries such as broken bones and strains.

These standard benefits were reduced by the Ontario government to help stabilize rates and give consumers more choice, but people are likely to feel the impact. How far does a million dollars go, spread over a lifetime, when the average price of a detached home in Toronto is $1.2 million?

Limits described under the earlier reform. Ask your broker for the limits on your own policy.

The good news: there are options

To be better prepared financially, you can increase, extend and buy additional accident benefit coverage from your insurance broker, depending on what you need: increasing coverage, extending it to cover minor injuries, or adding benefits to cover unexpected costs. If you earn more than $30,000 a year, you can increase your income replacement coverage to $600, $800 or $1,000 a week (depending on your income).

A quick online quote is convenient, but it won't give you the complete picture. Talk to your broker about what's changed in your life this past year, and how your family's life has changed. Instead of starting with price, start with your needs, then weigh them against your other expenses to decide what's most important.

Know your options! Talk to your insurance broker about customizing your auto insurance. It could be the best decision you've ever made.
