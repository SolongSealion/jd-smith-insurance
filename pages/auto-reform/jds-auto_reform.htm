Our advice

Personal vehicles

Before July 2026 you could already choose your liability limits, your collision deductible, the loss of use option and the replacement value option for new cars. The accident benefits, though, were standard coverage.

Now you have a choice of accident benefits and limits. We recommend you keep all the good coverage you have now, and perhaps consider increasing some of it.

Commercial or business use vehicles

Your employer may have reduced the accident benefit coverage for their drivers. Please check with your employer. You don't want to have an accident in the company truck or vehicle and then find out the coverage that protects you in a major accident was reduced.

We recommend that companies keep as much coverage as possible to protect their employees.

If you had a serious accident and could not work, you would want the best coverage possible. We tell our clients it is not worth removing coverage to save just a few dollars.

Changes to Ontario auto insurance

Ontario is updating its auto insurance rules to give you more control over your coverage. Starting July 1, 2026, some accident benefits that were once included in every auto policy become optional, so you can choose the coverage that best suits your needs.

What are Statutory Accident Benefits (SABs)?

Statutory Accident Benefits (SABs) can provide financial support to help cover costs if you are injured in a car accident. They can help pay for medical treatment, rehabilitation and income replacement, regardless of who caused the accident.

What's staying the same

  • Basic medical, rehabilitation and attendant care benefits remain included in all policies to help you recover after an accident.
  • You can still choose to buy or increase benefits and coverages, such as supplementary medical, rehabilitation, caregiver support and indexation benefits.

What's becoming optional

These accident benefits become optional. You can choose to remove or add them, for yourself or other covered persons, depending on your needs.

Income replacement
Helps replace income you or another covered person may lose because of an auto accident.
Non-earner
If you or another covered person is a student or unemployed and an accident keeps you from leading a normal life, this can provide financial support while you recover.
Caregiver benefits
Helps cover caregiving costs if you can no longer care for a household member who needs it, such as a child or aging parent.
Lost educational expenses
Helps cover the costs you lose if an accident keeps you from attending school or an education program.
Expenses of visitors
Helps cover reasonable and necessary expenses of visitors, such as a sibling or parents, while you recover.
Housekeeping and home maintenance
Helps cover costs if you can't do the housekeeping and home maintenance you normally did before the accident.
Damage to personal items
Helps repair or replace personal items damaged in an accident, such as clothing, prescription eyewear and hearing aids.
Death and funeral benefits
Compensates some family members, and helps cover some funeral costs, if you or another covered person dies because of an accident.

Who is affected by these changes

From July 1, 2026, the optional benefits apply only to:

  • The insured person
  • The insured person's spouse and dependents
  • Drivers listed on the policy
Some people who were covered before, such as pedestrians, cyclists and certain passengers, may no longer be eligible for these accident benefits.

What this means for you

Existing customers
Your policy renews with your current coverage unless you agree in writing with your insurer to decline the benefits or change them. However, who is covered for the newly optional benefits changes on July 1, 2026, regardless of your renewal date, as listed above. Review your coverage to see whether you need to make changes.
New customers
If you buy or renew a policy after July 1, 2026, you can choose the benefits you want. Be sure to ask your insurer or broker what is included in your quote.
Commercial customers
Speak to your insurer or broker to learn how this change affects you or your employees.

What should you do now?

  1. Review your current coverage. Understand which benefits are already included.
  2. Check your workplace benefits. You may have similar coverage through your employer or private insurance.
  3. Assess your needs. Consider what coverage will best meet your needs.
  4. Speak with your broker. We can help you understand these changes and decide what's right for you.
