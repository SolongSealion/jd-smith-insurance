What are Statutory Accident Benefits?

Statutory Accident Benefits (SABs) can provide financial support if you are injured in an auto accident. These benefits can be available regardless of who caused the accident, and can cover things like medical treatment, rehabilitation and income if you can't work because of your injuries.

What's changing?

Starting July 1, 2026, Ontario drivers have more choice and control over their coverage. Certain essential benefits remain mandatory, but many others are now optional and can be added or removed when you buy or renew your policy.

Existing customers

Your policy renews with the same coverage and limits, unless you agree with your insurer in writing to decline the benefits or change them. Before making changes, check whether your personal or work benefits already provide similar coverage.

Buying or renewing on or after July 1, 2026

You can choose which optional accident benefits you would like to purchase. Ask your broker what is included in your quote.

Essential accident benefits coverage remains

Standard medical, rehabilitation and attendant care benefits continue to be included in all auto insurance policies, regardless of who caused the accident. They cover medical expenses, therapy and personal care for injuries from an accident, including doctor visits, physiotherapy and help with daily activities like bathing and dressing.

Optional benefits you can now choose

These accident benefits are now optional. You can choose to remove or add them, for yourself or other covered persons, depending on your needs.

Optional benefitWhat it coversCoverage limits
Income replacementHelps replace income you or another covered person may lose because of an auto accident.Set by your insurer
Non-earnerIf you or another covered person is a student or unemployed and an auto accident keeps you from leading a normal life, this benefit can provide financial support while you recover.Set by your insurer
Caregiver benefitsHelps cover caregiving expenses if you or another covered person is injured and can no longer care for a household member who needs it, such as a child or aging parent.Set by your insurer
Lost educational expensesIf an auto accident keeps you or another covered person from attending school or an education program, this benefit can help cover the costs you have lost.Set by your insurer
Expenses of visitorsHelps cover reasonable and necessary expenses of visitors, such as a sibling or parents, if you or another covered person is injured in an auto accident.Set by your insurer
Housekeeping and home maintenanceHelps cover costs if you or another covered person can't do the housekeeping and home maintenance tasks normally done before an auto accident.Set by your insurer
Damage to personal itemsHelps cover the cost to repair or replace personal items (such as clothing, prescription eyewear and hearing aids) damaged in an auto accident.Set by your insurer
Death benefitsCompensates some family members if you or another covered person dies because of an auto accident.Set by your insurer
Funeral benefitsHelps cover some funeral costs if you or another covered person dies because of an auto accident.Set by your insurer

Coverage limits vary by insurer. Ask your broker for the limits that apply to your quote.

Benefits that remain optional

You can still choose to add these benefits to your policy:

Optional benefitWhat it coversCoverage limits
Supplementary medical, rehabilitation and attendant careHelps cover medical expenses beyond the standard limits.Set by your insurer
Dependant careHelps cover reasonable and necessary additional expenses of caring for dependants, such as a child, spouse or aging parent.Set by your insurer
IndexationAdjusts certain weekly benefit payments and monetary limits each year to reflect changes in the cost of living.Set by your insurer

Who is covered?

Existing policies renew with the same coverage and limits, but who is covered for the newly optional accident benefits changed on July 1, 2026, regardless of your renewal date. Optional accident benefits under your auto policy now cover only:

  • The named insured
  • The spouse of the named insured
  • Dependants of the named insured and of the named insured's spouse
  • People listed in the policy as drivers of the vehicle

What steps should I take?

  1. Review your current coverage to understand which benefits you have today in your auto insurance policy.
  2. Review coverage you may already have through your workplace, a private benefits plan, or your life and health insurance providers.
  3. Think about your needs. Consider which coverage is right for you. Our self assessment can help.
  4. Speak to your broker to learn more about your options.

Make informed decisions, and choose the benefits that best fit your needs and budget.
