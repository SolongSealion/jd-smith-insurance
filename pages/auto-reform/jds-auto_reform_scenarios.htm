Coverage that fits your life

Since July 1, 2026, Ontario auto insurance gives you more options and control over your coverage, so you can choose insurance that best meets your needs. To learn what each optional benefit covers, read the fact sheet.

It's also important to review your private or workplace benefits plans, as you may already be covered elsewhere.

These examples are for illustration only and don't reflect everyone's situation. Before changing your policy, think about your needs and circumstances, review your current coverage and speak to your broker.

Who are you most like?

Single parent Self-employed Retired Young family Tradesperson University student Newcomer to Canada Delivery driver Office worker
1. The single parent

Alice

Alice manages a busy schedule. They work as a contractor while caring for two young children and handling all the household responsibilities on their own. They rely on their car every day for work, school drop-offs and pick-ups, the kids' activities, errands and more. Without employer benefits or much savings, even a short recovery from an injury could make daily life difficult.

An auto accident could make it hard to keep up income, childcare and household routines, especially with no family nearby. Optional benefits may help them stay stable while they recover.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Income replacement: to help replace income lost because of an accident.
  • Caregiver: if they can no longer care for their children.
  • Expenses of visitors: for family, such as a sibling or parents, who come to help.
  • Housekeeping and home maintenance: if they can't keep up with tasks at home.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
  • Dependant care: for the extra costs of caring for dependants.
  • Indexation: so weekly payments and limits keep up with the cost of living.
2. The self-employed consultant

Bob

Bob runs an independent IT consulting business from home. Their mornings usually start with a coffee and a long drive to client sites across the province. With their spouse working part-time, the household relies mainly on Bob's income for the mortgage and household expenses. Being self-employed, they have no employer plan and haven't bought disability or life insurance.

If they were injured in an auto accident, working could become impossible and the household would face financial challenges. Personal items, such as their hearing aid, could also be damaged and need replacing.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Income replacement: to help replace income lost because of an accident.
  • Housekeeping and home maintenance: if they can't keep up with tasks at home.
  • Damage to personal items: to repair or replace items like a hearing aid or eyewear.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
  • Indexation: so weekly payments and limits keep up with the cost of living.
3. The retired surviving spouse

Charlie

Charlie is happily retired after a long career as a project manager. They live an active life, look after all the upkeep of their apartment, and drive themselves to medical appointments, errands and social activities. A pension is their only income. It covers the basics but leaves little room for unexpected costs such as paid household help or unplanned medical expenses.

If they were injured in an auto accident, they might need help with chores and home maintenance, and support from their children, who live out of town.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Non-earner: for financial support while they recover.
  • Expenses of visitors: for children or siblings who travel to help.
  • Housekeeping and home maintenance: if they can't keep up with tasks at home.
  • Damage to personal items: to repair or replace items like eyewear or hearing aids.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
  • Supplementary medical, rehabilitation and attendant care: for medical costs beyond the standard limits.
4. The young family

Dave & Eve

With two toddlers, Dave and Eve manage a busy schedule and a tight budget. Dave works in construction and drives long distances every day for work. Eve works part-time as a retail associate, is the main caregiver for the children and does most of the housekeeping for the townhouse they rent. They live paycheque to paycheque and have no disability or life insurance.

If either were injured in an auto accident, the family could face real financial hardship and trouble covering the caregiving.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Income replacement: to help replace income lost because of an accident.
  • Caregiver: if they can no longer care for their children.
  • Housekeeping and home maintenance: if they can't keep up with tasks at home.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
  • Dependant care: for the extra costs of caring for dependants.
  • Indexation: so weekly payments and limits keep up with the cost of living.
5. The tradesperson

Fred

Fred is an electrician and the main earner for their family. They drive to job sites across the region. The work is physically demanding, so even a moderate injury outside work could stop them from working. Their spouse is currently unemployed, so the family depends on Fred's income for the bills, the mortgage and household needs.

They have two active teenagers at home, both listed as drivers on the policy. The eldest just turned 19 and is in college, and an injury from an auto accident could mean dropping out of the program.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Income replacement: to help replace income lost because of an accident.
  • Lost educational expenses: if an accident keeps a student from school or college.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
  • Dependant care: for the extra costs of caring for dependants.
  • Indexation: so weekly payments and limits keep up with the cost of living.
6. The university student

Gina

At 21, Gina is in their final year of university and works part-time at a café. They rent an apartment near campus with three roommates, and rely on their car to get to school, work and activities.

If they were injured in an auto accident, they might not be able to continue their studies or keep their job. Their parents live in another city and would need to travel to care for them.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Income replacement: to help replace income lost because of an accident.
  • Non-earner: for financial support while they recover.
  • Lost educational expenses: if an accident keeps them from school.
  • Expenses of visitors: for parents who travel to help.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
7. The newcomer to Canada

Henrietta

Henrietta recently moved to Canada with their partner and young child, and they are settling into their community. They just started a new job as an administrative assistant and don't yet qualify for workplace disability benefits. Their partner works irregular hours while looking for more stable work. They have limited savings and rely on their car for work and childcare.

If an auto accident caused an injury, the household could struggle to cover lost income, childcare and household responsibilities.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Income replacement: to help replace income lost because of an accident.
  • Caregiver: if they can no longer care for their child.
  • Housekeeping and home maintenance: if they can't keep up with tasks at home.
  • Expenses of visitors: for family who come to help.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
  • Dependant care: for the extra costs of caring for dependants.
8. The delivery driver

Isaac

Isaac spends most days on the road, working full time for a local courier company. Off work, they relax at their apartment or spend time with their parents, who live nearby. On the job they drive a company-owned vehicle insured under a commercial policy. Their income depends entirely on being able to drive, so an injury in an accident at work could stop their earnings and make rent hard to pay.

The courier company carries only the mandatory accident benefits, because employers don't have to buy optional benefits on commercial policies. Adding optional benefits to Isaac's own personal policy could give peace of mind that there is income support if an accident happens.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Income replacement: to help replace income lost because of an accident.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
  • Indexation: so weekly payments and limits keep up with the cost of living.
9. The office worker

Justine

Justine works full time for a large company and lives in the suburbs with their spouse and three children. Their employer provides extended health and disability benefits, which already include some income, caregiving, dependant care and housekeeping protection.

Before adding optional benefits to their auto policy, it's important to review what the workplace plan already includes, to avoid overlap and make sure they have the right level of protection.

Optional benefits to consider

  • Income replacement: to help replace income lost because of an accident.
  • Death and funeral: to support family members and help cover funeral costs.
