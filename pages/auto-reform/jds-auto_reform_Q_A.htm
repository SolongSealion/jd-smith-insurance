About the changes

Ontario changed auto insurance to give drivers more choice and flexibility, so you can choose benefits to fit your needs. Medical, rehabilitation and attendant care benefits remain mandatory, so everyone has access to essential recovery support.

All other accident benefit coverages, such as income replacement, are now optional. Who is covered for these optional benefits under your policy also changed.

Standard medical, rehabilitation and attendant care benefits are still included in every auto insurance policy, regardless of who caused the accident. They cover medical expenses, therapy and personal care for injuries from an accident, including doctor visits, physiotherapy and help with daily activities like bathing and dressing.

All other accident benefits are now optional, including:

  • Income replacement: helps replace income you or another covered person may lose because of an auto accident.
  • Non-earner: financial support while you recover, if you or another covered person is a student or unemployed and an accident keeps you from leading a normal life.
  • Caregiver benefits: helps cover caregiving costs if an injured person can no longer care for a household member who needs it, such as a child or aging parent.
  • Lost educational expenses: helps cover the costs you lose if an accident keeps you from attending school or an education program.
  • Expenses of visitors: helps cover reasonable and necessary expenses of visitors, such as a sibling or parents.
  • Housekeeping and home maintenance: helps cover costs if you can't do the housekeeping and home maintenance you normally did before the accident.
  • Damage to personal items: helps repair or replace personal items damaged in an accident, such as clothing, prescription eyewear and hearing aids.
  • Death benefits: compensates some family members if you or another covered person dies because of an auto accident.
  • Funeral benefits: helps cover some funeral costs.

Supplementary medical, rehabilitation and attendant care benefits, dependant care benefits and indexation continue to be optional. See the fact sheet for the full list.

Yes. The changes apply to all drivers and auto insurance policies in Ontario, including personal private passenger, commercial, motorcycle, snowmobile and all-terrain vehicle policies.

Some Ontarians already pay for benefits through their employer or a private insurance plan. The reforms end the one-size-fits-all approach, so you no longer have to pay for benefits you already have elsewhere or don't want.

You can now tailor your auto insurance to your situation: skip optional benefits you don't need, and add the ones that give you and your family extra protection.

If you remove benefits that are now optional from your existing coverage, it may change your premium.

Our advice: if you had a serious accident and could not work, you would want the best coverage possible. It is not worth removing coverage to save just a few dollars.

Who is covered

Since July 1, 2026, the newly optional benefits under your auto policy apply only to:

  • The named insured
  • The spouse of the named insured
  • Dependants of the named insured and of the named insured's spouse
  • People listed in the policy as drivers of the vehicle

Standard accident benefits still apply to all passengers. Optional accident benefits apply only to the named insured, their spouse, dependants of the named insured and of the named insured's spouse, and listed drivers. A passenger injured in an accident is not covered for optional benefits unless they fall into one of these groups under an auto policy that includes optional benefits.

Standard accident benefits still apply to uninsured pedestrians and cyclists. Optional accident benefits apply only to the named insured, their spouse, dependants of the named insured and of the named insured's spouse, and listed drivers. A pedestrian or cyclist injured in an accident is not covered for optional benefits unless they fall into one of these groups under an auto policy that includes optional benefits.

You may be covered for some accident benefits when renting a car. Review your auto insurance policy and the rental contract to understand what you're covered for, or ask your broker.

Existing and new policies

Yes. For renewing customers, all accident benefits except medical, rehabilitation and attendant care are now optional. Your policy renews automatically with your current coverage and limits, unless you agree with your insurer in writing to decline certain benefits or change them. You can review and update your coverage based on your needs. Before making changes, check whether your personal or work benefits already provide similar coverage.

Your policy renews with the same coverage and limits, but who is covered for the newly optional accident benefits changed on July 1, 2026, regardless of your renewal date.

If you buy or renew a policy on or after July 1, 2026, you can choose which optional accident benefits you would like to purchase. Ask your broker which benefits are included in your quote. You may want to add optional benefits based on your needs.

Choosing and changing your benefits

Every policyholder can add or remove optional accident benefits from their policy.

Before you remove or add optional benefits:

  1. Review your current coverage (if you are an existing customer). Know what benefits you already have through your auto policy.
  2. Review your workplace or private benefits plan. You may already have coverage through your employer, a private plan, or your life and health insurance providers.
  3. Think about your needs. Consider which coverage is right for you, and whether opting out is practical.
  4. Speak to your broker. We can explain your options for your auto insurance coverage.

Claims

There is no impact on ongoing claims. The auto insurance coverage you had at the time of the accident applies to your claim.

You may have coverage through both. Since July 1, 2026, auto insurance pays first for medical or rehabilitation benefits (except medication costs) for any injury from an auto accident.
