Winter storage tips

It’s time for winter storage preparation. A few simple steps now will prevent any springtime heartbreak for your car, classic or motorcycle.

See the steps Keep your insurance
Before you park it

How to prepare your vehicle

If a car is simply parked in the garage for months without preparation, you may come back to a dead battery or, even worse, a damaged engine, flattened tires, or electrical or interior damage from rodents. These steps will prevent any springtime heartbreak.

Don’t cancel your insurance while the car is parked. See why.
  1. Wash and wax
  2. Use a car cover
  3. Change the oil
  4. Fill the fuel tank
  5. Look after the battery
  6. Leave the parking brake off
  7. Take care of the tires
  8. Repel rodents

It may seem fruitless to wash the car when it is about to be put away for months, but it is an easy step that shouldn’t be overlooked. Water stains or bird droppings left on the car can permanently damage the paint. Clean the wheels and the undersides of the fenders to get rid of mud, grease and tar. For added protection, give the car a coat of wax and treat any interior leather with a good conditioner.

Even though your classic is stored in the garage in semi-stable temperatures and protected from the elements, a weatherproof car cover will keep any spills or dust off the paint. It can also protect from scratches while you move objects around the parked car.

If you will be storing the vehicle for longer than 30 days, consider getting the oil changed. Used engine oil has contaminants that could damage the engine or lead to sludge buildup.

Before long-term storage of over 30 days, fill the gas tank to prevent moisture from accumulating inside the fuel tank and to keep the seals from drying out. Add a fuel stabilizer to prevent ethanol buildup and protect the engine from gum, varnish and rust. The stabilizer will keep the gas from deteriorating for up to 12 months.

An unattended battery will eventually lose its charge, and you may have to buy a new one in the spring. The easiest, low-tech solution is to disconnect the battery cables: the negative (ground) first, then the positive. You’ll likely lose your stereo presets, time and other settings. To keep them, and to make sure the car starts the moment you return, use a battery tender (a trickle charger). It hooks up to the battery and plugs into a wall outlet, and delivers just enough power to keep the battery from dying.

For general use it is a good idea to use the parking brake, but not when you leave a car in storage long term: if the brake pads touch the rotors for an extended time, they could fuse together. Use a tire stopper or two (also called a chock) to keep the car from moving instead.

A vehicle left stationary too long can develop flat spots where its weight presses down on the treads. This happens faster in the cold, especially with high-performance or low-profile tires, and in severe cases the flat spot becomes permanent and the tire must be replaced. If your car will be stored for more than 30 days, consider taking the wheels off and placing the car on jack stands at all four corners. It is more work than simply inflating the tires to the recommended pressure, but it can save you a set of destroyed tires.

A solid garage keeps your car dry and relatively warm, which can also attract rodents in the cold winter months. Cover any gaps a mouse could get through, such as the exhaust pipe or an air intake; steel wool works well. Spread mothballs, or cotton swabs dipped in peppermint oil, around the vehicle as a repellent (a product called Fresh Cab is a less smelly option). You can also lay down a few mousetraps, but make sure someone checks the garage regularly.

Maintain insurance

Don’t cancel your insurance while your car is in storage

You might be tempted to cancel your auto insurance when your vehicle is in storage in order to save money. If you remove coverage completely, it would be a major loss if a fire started, the weight of snow caused your roof to collapse, or a theft occurred.

Even if just collision coverage is temporarily removed, the car would not be covered if another moving object (for example a tractor or motorcycle) rolled into it, or if the car were to fall off its jack stands.

Classic car insurance: if you have classic car insurance, the J.D. Smith policy normally takes winter storage into account in your annual premium, so you don’t have to worry about your car over the winter months.

Still covered while it’s parked

  • Fire in the garage
  • A roof collapse under the weight of snow
  • Theft
  • Something rolling into the car, or the car falling off its jack stands (if you keep collision)

Ask your broker what coverage makes sense for your vehicle before you store it.
