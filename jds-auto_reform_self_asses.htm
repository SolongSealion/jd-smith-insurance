How to use the checklist

Since July 1, 2026, Ontario auto insurance gives you more flexibility and control over your coverage. The fact sheet explains each optional benefit and who it covers.

  1. Read each set of questions carefully.
  2. Tick the box if your answer is "yes" for you or anyone else covered under your policy. Leave it blank if the answer is "no".
  3. If you tick one or more boxes in a section, that benefit may be worth considering. Your list builds up as you go.
  4. Speak to your broker to review your coverage and learn more about your options.
Before you begin
If you have a private or workplace benefits plan, you may already have coverage for some of these benefits. Before changing the optional benefits on your auto policy, review your plan or speak with your benefits advisor to confirm you have the right coverage.

Self-assessment checklist

1. Income replacement Worth considering

What it covers: Helps replace income you or another covered person may lose because of an auto accident.

2. Non-earner Worth considering

What it covers: If you or another covered person is a student or unemployed and an auto accident keeps you from leading a normal life, this benefit can provide financial support while you recover.

3. Caregiver Worth considering

What it covers: Helps cover caregiving expenses if you or another covered person is injured and can no longer care for a household member who needs it, such as a child or aging parent.

4. Lost educational expenses Worth considering

What it covers: If an auto accident keeps you or another covered person from attending school or an education program, this benefit can help cover the costs you have lost.

5. Expenses of visitors Worth considering

What it covers: Helps cover reasonable and necessary expenses of visitors, such as a sibling or parents, if you or another covered person is injured in an auto accident.

6. Housekeeping and home maintenance Worth considering

What it covers: Helps cover costs if you or another covered person can't do the housekeeping and home maintenance tasks normally done before an auto accident.

7. Damage to personal items Worth considering

What it covers: Helps cover the cost to repair or replace personal items (such as clothing, prescription eyewear and hearing aids) damaged in an auto accident.

8. Death benefit Worth considering

What it covers: Compensates some family members if you or another covered person dies because of an auto accident.

9. Funeral benefit Worth considering

What it covers: Helps cover some funeral costs if you or another covered person dies because of an auto accident.

10. Supplementary medical, rehabilitation and attendant care Worth considering

What it covers: Helps cover medical expenses beyond the standard limits.

11. Dependant care Worth considering

What it covers: Helps cover reasonable and necessary additional expenses of caring for dependants, such as a child, spouse or aging parent.

12. Indexation Worth considering

What it covers: Adjusts certain weekly payments and monetary limits each year to reflect changes in the cost of living.
